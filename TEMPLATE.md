# Merlin — Gold-Standard Workbook Template

Every Merlin research run produces a workbook with these seven tabs, in this order.
The architecture mirrors two reference outreach workbooks (ski-resort US and Canada
editions), generalised for any industry.
Copy this structure into Google Sheets or Excel: one tab per section, header row first.

## Tab 1 — About
Purpose of the workbook, scope, research date, headline counts (main contact records,
unique emails, primary / secondary contacts, held records, directory entries, regions
represented, areas without email), how to use the list, the publication-evidence note,
the verification boundary ("published does not mean delivery-tested"), the SENDING
RULES block (one sender only; the workbook is the only queue; read Notes before
sending; DO NOT RESEND is binding; never a third attempt to a twice-failed address;
drafting-safe check before any send; pace sends; stop on the first limit notice;
update the row after every outcome), and the OPERATOR ATTRIBUTION note: every send is
recorded under the name of the person who made it (the owner or any operator on the team) in
the Sent by column and the Activity log's Recorded by column, in the same pass.

## Tab 2 — Contacts (master list)
Business name | Region | Operator / client | Published email | Suggested greeting | Contact use | Address / branches | Email source URL | Evidence type | Location source URL | Notes | Checked | Outreach status | Shared inbox | Record ID | Contact ID | Contact name | Job title / department | Eligibility | Eligibility reason | Verification date | Outreach stage | Last sent date | Sent by | Total messages sent | Latest delivery evidence | Last reply date | Reply outcome | Next action | Next action date | Owner | Instagram handle | IG verification | IG verification notes

One row per unique published email. Notes preserve status history — append, never
overwrite. Outreach columns are filled in by whichever operator makes contact, in the
same pass as the contact. The final three columns (Instagram handle, IG verification,
IG verification notes) mirror the Directory tab's Instagram fields inline so the flat
Contacts export works as a standalone CRM file; in the workbook they also live on the
Directory tab, keyed by Record ID.

## Tab 3 — Directory (every business found, contacted or not)
Business name | Region | Operator / client | Official website | Published emails | Phone | Address / location | Email source URL | Evidence type | Operating status | Notes | Checked | Record ID | Instagram handle | IG verification | IG verification notes

IG verification is exactly one of: **Verified** (confirmed as the business's own
account from an official source), **Probable** (strong candidate, unconfirmed — the
doubt is explained in the notes), **Not found** (searched; no official account
established — a fan, template or unrelated account is not a handle).

## Tab 4 — Held contacts
Business name | Region | Operator / client | Published email | Suggested greeting | Contact use | Address / branches | Email source URL | Evidence type | Location source URL | Notes | Checked | Outreach status | Shared inbox | Record ID

Prospects held back (closed, paused, ineligible, unverifiable) with the reason in
Notes and Outreach status. Held means parked with a reason, never deleted.

## Tab 5 — Coverage rollup
Region | Directory entries | Unique contacts | Primary contacts | Areas without email

## Tab 6 — Activity log
Activity ID | Contact ID | Business / operator ID | Date and time | Event type | Campaign / purpose | Subject | Message or thread link | Sample / creative version | Outcome | Failure code / explanation | Recorded by | Notes

Every action on the workbook, attributed with Recorded by.

## Tab 7 — Suppression register
Email address or domain | Scope | Reason | Date recorded | Evidence link | Review condition

Addresses and domains that must never be contacted (dead domains, double failures,
do-not-contact). A suppression earned in one workbook is honoured in every other
workbook where the same address appears.
